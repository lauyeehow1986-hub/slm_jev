# notes_v5 — held-out Singapore-style clinical/admin notes with gold spans.
# SYNTHETIC: NOT REAL PATIENT DATA. Every person, name, NRIC/FIN, MRN, phone number, address, email and health detail below is invented for testing; any match to a real person, number or mailbox is coincidental.
# Author note: written from general knowledge by an author who did NOT see the system under test, its rules, its code, or any earlier evaluation sets.
# Institution, ward, company and ministry names are invented or used only as decoys (not identifiers).
#
# FORMAT
# - A note starts with a header line "=== v5_nNN | <kind> ===". The note text is every line after it up to the next "===" header line.
# - A gold span is written inline as {{label|text}}. The note text contains only "text"; the braces and "label|" are markup.
# - Spans are never nested; span text never contains "{{", "}}" or "|", and has no leading/trailing spaces.
# - Anything not inside {{...}} is not an identifier.
#
# LABELS
# - identifiers: name, national_id, mrn, case_visit, address, postal_code, phone, fax, email, dob, date_of_death, device, biometric, photo, other_id
# - SHI: hiv_sti, mental_health, substance_use, genetic, reproductive_sexual, other_sensitive
# - other: date_other (any date of the patient's care/events that is not a DOB or date of death; document/letter/message dates count)
#
# CONVENTIONS
# - Honorifics/titles (Dr, Mr, Mrs, Ms, Mdm, SN, SSN, ASP, Prof, A/Prof, MO, EMT, etc.) are OUTSIDE name spans. Every person is named, including staff, relatives, employers and callers.
# - "Singapore" and the postal code are OUTSIDE address spans; the postal code is its own postal_code span and keeps an "S" prefix when written like "S431002".
# - national_id covers NRIC/FIN/passport/birth certificate numbers, including masked, lower-case or spaced forms.
# - mrn = hospital record numbers; case_visit = visit/admission/encounter/case numbers of an episode; device = device serials and vehicle plates;
#   other_id = other identifying numbers (policy, claim, accession, practitioner registration, police report, bank account, employee, LPA, court case, specimen, blood unit numbers).
# - photo = image/photo file names or photo IDs of the person; biometric = biometric template IDs.
# - Emails use only reserved example.com / example.org / example.net domains. Every email address (personal or institutional) is labelled email.
# - Decoys left unmarked: institution/company/ministry names, ward/bed/room numbers, lab values, doses, times, ICD codes, device model numbers,
#   lot numbers, protocol/form/document numbers, generic dates not about a person's care (e.g. policy effective dates).
# - Notes v5_n05, v5_n13, v5_n19, v5_n24 and v5_n30 are negative controls with no identifiers.
#
# DELIBERATELY INVALID ID
# - v5_n21: "S7712345A" is an NRIC-shaped national_id with a deliberately WRONG check letter (the valid letter would be H); it is still labelled national_id.
# - Masked IDs ("SXXXX182B", "S****441A") are labelled national_id but are not checksum-testable.

=== v5_n05 | ward protocol ===
HYPOGLYCAEMIA MANAGEMENT IN ADULT INPATIENTS - Nursing Protocol NUR-PRO-0217, version 4.2
Effective 1 July 2025; next review June 2027. Applies to all adult wards (5A-8C) and the Community Hospital.
1. If capillary blood glucose (CBG) is below 4.0 mmol/L and the patient is conscious and able to swallow, give 15 g fast-acting carbohydrate (e.g. 150 mL fruit juice or 3 glucose tablets).
2. Recheck CBG after 15 minutes. Repeat step 1 up to 3 times.
3. If the patient is drowsy, NBM or unable to swallow: give IV dextrose 40% 50 mL via a patent cannula, or IM glucagon 1 mg if no IV access.
4. If GCS drops below 9 or seizures occur, activate Code Blue and inform the ward MO.
5. Once CBG is above 4.0 mmol/L, give a long-acting carbohydrate snack (2 biscuits or 1 slice of bread).
6. Document on the Hypoglycaemia Chart (form MR-114) and review the insulin or sulphonylurea dose with the primary team.
ICD-10 reference: E16.2. Audit indicator: time from CBG reading to treatment under 10 minutes.

=== v5_n24 | teaching note ===
TEACHING NOTE - Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA): first-hour priorities
Case vignette: a 19-year-old student presents with vomiting, abdominal pain and Kussmaul breathing. Glucose 28 mmol/L, pH 7.08, bicarbonate 8, ketones 5.2.
Diagnostic criteria: ketones 3.0 mmol/L or more, bicarbonate below 15 or pH below 7.3, and glucose above 11 or known diabetes.
Fluids: 0.9% saline 1 L over the first hour, then 1 L over 2 hours, adjusted to haemodynamics.
Insulin: fixed-rate IV infusion at 0.1 units/kg/h; continue basal insulin.
Potassium: add 40 mmol/L if K is 3.5 to 5.5; senior review if below 3.5.
Pitfall: never stop insulin because glucose falls; add dextrose 10% when glucose drops below 14.
Coding: E10.10. Reference: departmental guideline, revised March 2025.
