# notes_v21 — held-out Singapore-style clinical/admin notes with gold spans.
# SYNTHETIC: NOT REAL PATIENT DATA. Every person, name, NRIC/FIN, MRN, phone number, address, email and health detail below is invented for testing; any match to a real person, number or mailbox is coincidental.
# Author: writer F. Written from general knowledge only, by an author who did not see the system under test, its rules, its code, or any earlier evaluation sets.
# Theme of this set: patient- and family-written text and cross-border records (portal messages, emails, voicemails, handwritten notes, social posts, translated overseas letters, travel, maritime, dormitory, agency, school/club, probate, crowdfunding).
# FORMAT: a note starts with a header line "=== v21_nNN | <kind> ===". The note text is every line after it up to the next "===" header line.
# FORMAT: a gold span is written inline as {{label|text}}. The note text contains only "text"; braces and "label|" are markup. Spans are never nested, never contain "{{", "}}" or "|", and have no leading/trailing spaces. Anything not inside {{...}} is not an identifier.
# LABELS (identifiers): name, national_id, mrn, case_visit, address, postal_code, phone, fax, email, dob, date_of_death, device, biometric, photo, other_id
# LABELS (SHI): hiv_sti, mental_health, substance_use, genetic, reproductive_sexual, other_sensitive
# LABELS (other): date_other = any date of the patient's care/events that is not a DOB or date of death.
# CONVENTION: honorifics and titles (Dr, Mr, Mrs, Ms, Mdm, SN, SSN, Prof, Encik, Puan, Pn, Ny., Tn., dr., Aling, Maung, U, Daw, Kak, Uncle, Coach, Capt., C/O, DR.) are OUTSIDE name spans. A name span covers the full name as written (incl. bin/binti/s/o/d/o/a/l forms).
# CONVENTION: every mention of a person is labelled name, including repeats, surnames alone after a title, given names alone, nicknames, chat display names and INITIALS (in any form, incl. lower case). Kinship words used alone (Ah Ma, Bapak, papa, mum) are not names.
# CONVENTION: institution, hospital, clinic, company, law-firm, ship-operator and ministry NAMES are decoys and never marked, even when they contain surnames (e.g. a law firm named after partners).
# CONVENTION: EVERY phone number, fax number, email address, street address and postal code is marked wherever it appears, including letterheads, footers, switchboards, hotlines, department faxes and generic mailboxes. Company/organisation registration numbers (UEN, ROC, ROS, company reg., agency licence) are other_id.
# CONVENTION: field captions (NRIC, FIN, MRN, Tel, Fax, HP, DID, UEN, IC, Mob, Ph) are outside spans; only the value is labelled. Phone extensions are inside the phone span when written with the number. Phone numbers spoken in words, partly masked, used as a chat sender or as a PayNow proxy are still phone.
# CONVENTION: "Singapore" and country names are outside address spans; for overseas addresses the city/state/province named after the street line is also outside. The postal code is its own postal_code span (foreign postcodes too) and keeps an "S" prefix inside the span when written like "S543268".
# CONVENTION: national_id = NRIC/FIN/birth certificate/passport/MyKad/NIK/NRC/Aadhaar/PRC resident ID/birth registration no. mrn = hospital record numbers (incl. overseas UHID/RN/hospital no./clinic file no.). case_visit = visit/admission/outpatient/IP/registration numbers of an episode. device = device serial numbers, vehicle plates, vessel IMO number and call sign. other_id = everything else identifying (letter refs about the patient, claim/policy/estimate refs, practitioner and translator registration numbers, handles, profile URLs, bank accounts, court/probate refs, LPA refs, certificate numbers, employee numbers, seaman's book numbers, permit IPA numbers).
# CONVENTION: decoys left UNMARKED: organisation names, ward/bed/room numbers (including dormitory block-room codes), queue numbers, lab values, drug doses, times, ICD codes, device model names, drug/vaccine batch and lot numbers, SOP/form/clause/package codes, flight numbers, GPS positions, prices, generic dates not about a person's care.
# CONVENTION: the date of a letter, email or chat message that is about a specific patient is date_other. A relative date written as a date ("Sun 12 Jan", "15th") is date_other.
# CONVENTION (SHI): ordinary diagnoses (diabetes, TB, fracture, stroke, cancer, concussion) are not SHI. Dementia/Alzheimer's, depression, psychosis, self-harm and suicidality are mental_health; abuse, assault and suspected non-accidental injury are other_sensitive.
# NRIC/FIN checksum: weights 2,7,6,5,4,3,2; +4 for T/G, +3 for M; r = sum mod 11; S/T -> JZIHGFEDCBA, F/G -> XWUTRQPNMLK, M -> XWUTRQPNJLK. All NRIC/FIN-shaped values are valid (spaces ignored) EXCEPT one deliberate error.
# DELIBERATELY INVALID: in v21_n03 the NRIC "S7369393E" (as read out by a relative) has a wrong check letter (valid would end in D); it is still labelled national_id.
# Masked values (with X or *) cannot be checksum-validated and are still labelled with their identifier type. MCR numbers like "M14922A" are practitioner registrations (other_id), not FINs.
# NEGATIVE CONTROLS (no identifiers): v21_n06, v21_n12, v21_n19, v21_n25, v21_n30.

=== v21_n06 | patient-education leaflet (dengue) ===
DENGUE: WHAT TO WATCH FOR AT HOME
Leaflet PE-114, version 3 (reviewed March 2024)

Most people with dengue recover in 1-2 weeks. There is no specific antiviral treatment.

Do:
- Drink at least 2-3 litres of fluids a day (water, isotonic drinks, soup).
- Take paracetamol 500 mg, 1-2 tablets every 6 hours if needed (max 8 tablets in 24 hours).
- Rest and use mosquito repellent so you do not spread the virus.

Do NOT take ibuprofen, aspirin or other NSAIDs; they increase bleeding risk.

Return to the Emergency Department immediately if you have: severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding gums or nose, black stools, drowsiness or restlessness, or cold clammy hands. Days 3-7 of illness are the critical phase, even if the fever has gone.

Your platelet count will be checked at each review. A level below 20 x10^9/L may need admission.

Q: Do I need a referral letter from my home doctor?
